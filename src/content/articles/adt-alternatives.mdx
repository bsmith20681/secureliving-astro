There are a couple of great options for home security. My #1 pick is Vivint because it has all the same features, plus a few extras. If you want something that’s a lot more affordable, Cove is a great option.

## What To Look For In A Replacement

For a good replacement, here are a few key features that you’re going to want.

1. Security system that is monitored through a cellular connection.
2. Security system with two-way voice through the keypad.
3. Security cameras that you can access remotely (see live video, watch recorded video).
4. A system that has smart home capabilities.

The first two are the ones that matter most, and they’re the two that most of the cheaper home security systems drop first. If your power or internet goes out, cellular monitoring keeps the panel connected to the monitoring center. And if someone breaks in while you’re home, you’re not going to be in a position to pick up your phone, so an agent who can talk to you through the panel can confirm the emergency and get police moving faster.

## Vivint: The Closest Match

<figure>
  <Image src={vivintPanel} alt="Vivint touchscreen smart panel" />
  <figcaption>The Vivint smart hub. Two-way voice is built in, and you can watch your cameras on the panel.</figcaption>
</figure>

Vivint is probably the closest match because it has all the same features, plus a few more. My wife and I have had Vivint in our house for a little over three years now, so this is the home security company I’ve lived with the longest.

### Superb Monitoring Service And Two-Way Voice

First off, Vivint offers 24/7 monitoring to police, fire, and medical alert calls. They have a nice touchscreen keypad, and it works through a cellular connection, meaning it doesn’t matter if your power or Wi-Fi goes out, your house will still be protected.

They also have two-way talk built inside of the keypad. So if you’re in the house when a break-in occurs, you won’t be in a position to pick up and answer your phone. But the Vivint agent can talk to you through the alarm panel and get the information they need to dispatch to police quickly.

The equipment list looks familiar: door and window sensors, motion sensors, a glass break sensor, and the touchscreen hub. One small thing Vivint does better is the panel itself. You can watch *every* camera right on it. On my ADT panel, the newer Nest cameras don’t show up, so I had to grab my phone.

### Cameras With “Deter” Mode

The Vivint cameras are some of the best security cameras I’ve tested. Not only is the video quality excellent, they have a cool feature called “[Deter Mode](/home-security-systems/what-is-deter-mode-on-vivint/)”.

If someone walks in front of a Vivint camera, you’ll get an alert on your phone (just like the Nest cameras), but the Vivint camera will also whistle at the person, and an LED light around the lens will start to flash. If you have the floodlight attachment, it will also light up your whole front or back yard.

If you’re a burglar and you know you’re being recorded, you’re probably not going to stick around for long!

Another great feature about the Vivint cameras is that they will record without Wi-Fi. All the major security companies (SimpliSafe, Ring, etc) will record in short video clips. Both ADT and Vivint have the option to upgrade to 24/7 continuous recording (you record every minute of every hour of every day). It’s **$10** extra with ADT and **$7** with Vivint.

Both sets of cameras will record video clips without Wi-Fi. The Nest cameras will only record up to one hour, whereas the Vivint cameras will record everything and send it to the panel, even if you don’t have Wi-Fi. I cover that in more depth in [does Vivint work without Wi-Fi](/home-security-systems/does-vivint-work-without-wifi/).

Vivint also has a native power over Ethernet option, so you can plug the cameras straight into your router. If you’ve had cameras in the past that constantly buffered or dropped the connection, that alone is worth a look. The one catch is that the cameras are proprietary. If you ever cancel the service, you lose access to them in the app.

### Smart Home Automation Capabilities

If you want to control smart home devices like your lights, thermostats, or door locks, both systems can connect to any Z-Wave device. So on paper, they appear to be the same.

But the way that the Vivint app is set up and how granular you can get with the smart home automations is far superior. It’s not until you have the app in your hand that you start to see the differences.

Say you want an automation that runs Monday through Friday at 9 PM: arm the panel, turn off the lights, and lock the doors. You can build that in either app, but the flow in Vivint’s is better designed, and you get more triggers and actions per device. ADT will handle basic automations fine. If you want the house to feel like a *true* smart home, Vivint is the better choice. My [Vivint vs ADT review](/home-security-systems/vivint-vs-adt/) goes through the differences screen by screen.

### Installation, Pricing, And Contract

Vivint is professionally installed. When I recorded my video review, Vivint was running a promotion for a **free doorbell camera and free installation**.

With this much equipment, I’m glad it’s a pro installation. My tech ran the camera wiring behind the baseboards so you can’t see it anywhere in the house. Some of the outdoor cameras connect to internal wiring, which means drilling through exterior walls, and that’s *not* a Saturday project I’d want. Vivint’s techs can also take over existing hardwired sensors and some wireless sensors from other brands, which saves you money if your house already has them.

Monitoring starts at **$29.99 per month** for core monitoring and the app. Adding home automation brings it to $39, and each camera adds $5. With two cameras and automation, I pay $49 monthly. Equipment packages start around $199, and a full setup with several cameras can run a little over $1,000.

I’m not a fan of contracts, so I like that if you purchase all the equipment up front, the service is **month-to-month (no contract)**. Or, you can finance the equipment (0% interest) over 42 or 60 months. While there’s a balance on the equipment, you’re committed to the service, and if you cancel early you pay off whatever is left.

## Cove: The Most Affordable Home Security System

<figure>
  <Image src={coveSystem} alt="Complete Cove security system with Eufy cameras" />
  <figcaption>The full Cove system I installed, including three Eufy cameras.</figcaption>
</figure>

Cove is a little more affordable than the other two, and it doesn’t skimp on any of the core security features. Most budget home security systems save you money by dropping the touchscreen panel or the two-way voice. Cove kept both, which is the *whole* reason it’s on this list.

### Reliable Monitoring, Great Panel, And Good Cameras

Cove has a nice touchscreen panel (just like the other two) that has two-way voice built in. It’s monitored 24/7 to police, fire, and medical alert calls. The panel connects to the monitoring center via your Wi-Fi network, but it has a cellular backup. So if your power and internet go out, you’ll still have monitoring.

<figure>
  <Image src={covePanelHeld} alt="Cove touchscreen panel held in hands" />
  <figcaption>Cove’s touchscreen panel. Two-way voice is built in.</figcaption>
</figure>

The entry sensors, motion sensors, and glass break sensor are all wireless. Cove also sells life safety devices like a smoke detector, a carbon monoxide detector, and a flood sensor, so the monitoring center can watch for more than break-ins. For your family’s safety I’d add at least the smoke and CO safety sensors.

The cameras are really nice too. You get all the same features that you get with the Nest and Vivint cameras (live view, two-way talk, recording). The Cove cameras will also record without Wi-Fi (they have a built-in SD card).

<figure>
  <Image src={coveOutdoorCam} alt="Cove outdoor camera daytime view of a backyard pool" />
  <figcaption>Daytime footage from my Cove outdoor camera.</figcaption>
</figure>

The only down side to the Cove cameras is that they only record video clips. There is no option to upgrade to 24/7 continuous recording like there is with the other two. Cove also has no home automation, so if controlling your lights and locks from the app is a must, go with Vivint.

One upside people overlook: the cameras are made by Eufy, so they still work if you ever cancel. You just sync them to the Eufy app. That’s the same reason I like the Nest cameras.

### Installation And Pricing

Cove is a DIY system. It took me about three hours to install it. When I recorded my video review, they had a promotion going on for 70% off all equipment and a free indoor camera. I paid about **$250 up front** for my setup.

The security side of the installation was the *easy* part. You plug the hub into your router, plug in the panel, and the panel walks you through syncing each sensor. The sensors use 3M adhesive, so you peel off the backing and stick them on. That part took me about 30 minutes, and the sensors come off cleanly, which matters if you rent. The cameras were the frustrating part. Getting the outdoor camera to sync took *most* of the afternoon, but once it connected I never had a problem with it again.

<figure>
  <Image src={coveAdhesive} alt="Peeling the 3M adhesive backing off a Cove sensor" />
  <figcaption>Cove’s sensors stick on with 3M adhesive. No drilling.</figcaption>
</figure>

Cove starts at just **$23 monthly** for home security monitoring. When you add cameras, it goes up to **$33 monthly**. Of that $33, $5 goes towards a credit on your account that you can use to purchase more equipment. You can also add as many cameras as you want without the bill going up.

So for about **$28** monthly out of pocket, you get a really nice setup with cameras. There’s no contract, so there’s *nothing* to get out of later.

## Why SimpliSafe And Ring Are Only “Honorable Mentions”

You’ll see the SimpliSafe and Ring systems on a lot of the “ADT alternative” lists online. The reason that I’m not including them in this article is that they don’t have two-way talk through the panel.

The other reason is that the cameras on both these systems do not record if you don’t have Wi-Fi.

Both are popular home security systems, and each has something unique. SimpliSafe has a really nice feature called “Active Guard”, where a representative at the monitoring station will monitor your outdoor cameras on your behalf, and if they see someone doing something sketchy, they can talk to them through the camera, and even dispatch to the police.

The tradeoffs with SimpliSafe are the basic push-button keypad, proprietary cameras, and limited automation. Basic monitoring now starts at $32.99, so it’s no longer the budget option it used to be. Ring is probably the most affordable home security option on the market, and they make some great cameras. Monitoring starts at $20 monthly, and smoke and CO monitoring is another $5. It makes the most sense if you already own Ring cameras and want to add basic monitoring around them. There’s no touchscreen, and the automations need third-party apps. ADT and Vivint, the two pro-installed systems, are similar in that you’ll end up paying a bit more monthly, but you get a more full-featured system. Cove and the DIY line, the two DIY systems, are a lot more affordable, but you’re kind of just getting a more traditional alarm system.

That’s a *real* advantage over Vivint’s $5 per camera if you want five or six of them. Cove costs about **$20 less monthly** than ADT, which works out to around $240 a year, and that’s why it’s my pick for home security on a budget.

## What Happens To Your Equipment When You Switch Providers?

If you’re under contract, I’d highly recommend reading the terms of your contract to see what you’re getting yourself into.

If you paid for the equipment up front, you’re locked in for the first year. If you financed it, you’re committed until the balance is paid off, so waiting until the term ends is the cheapest way out.

If you have Nest cameras, you can still use them after you cancel (you just use the Google Home app).

If you have an existing wireless or hardwired security system in your home, both Vivint and ADT can take over those sensors (as long as they’re in good working order).

Here’s what happens to the rest of the gear once you leave:

- Cove and SimpliSafe only work with their own sensors, so you’ll buy new ones with either.
- Z-Wave locks, thermostats, and light switches carry over to Vivint.
- Cove can’t use any of your Z-Wave devices.
- Vivint’s techs can usually take over existing hardwired sensors and some wireless sensors, which cuts your equipment cost.

If you just bought a house with the equipment already installed, my guide on [buying a house with ADT](/home-security-systems/bought-a-house-with-adt/) walks through your options.

## Which One Should You Buy?

If you want all the features that ADT offers, plus a few more, and you *don’t* want to be tied to a contract, get **Vivint**.

If you want to save a few dollars per month and you don’t care about the “smart home” stuff, get **Cove**.

If you already have ADT, and you have a *lot* of cameras (5 or more) and just want to be able to control a couple smart devices around the house, then just stay put.
