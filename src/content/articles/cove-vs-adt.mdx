I've reviewed both the Cove and ADT home security systems individually, so I wanted to put them head to head and break down exactly how they compare. These are two very different systems built for two very different people, and the right pick depends on what you care about most.

The quick take: ADT is the better system overall. It has professional installation, smart home automation, straight cellular monitoring, and six monitoring centers across the U.S. But it costs more and locks you into a contract. Cove is the budget-friendly DIY option that still covers all the security basics, with no contract and a much lower price tag. If you just need solid home security without the extras, Cove gets the job done for a lot less money.

Here's the full comparison.

## Cove vs ADT Monitoring Plans

Both systems offer professional monitoring that dispatches police, fire, and medical. But there are some real differences in how each system communicates with the monitoring center.

Cove connects through your home Wi-Fi first and uses cellular as a backup. So if your internet goes down, it falls back to cellular and you still have service. ADT goes straight to cellular as the primary connection. It doesn't rely on your Wi-Fi at all. That means if your power goes out and your internet drops, ADT is already on cellular without needing to switch over. I didn't have issues with either system during testing, but ADT's approach gives me more confidence. In a real emergency, you want the fastest possible connection to the monitoring center, and skipping the Wi-Fi step matters.

ADT also has six monitoring centers spread across the U.S. If one center goes down, five others can pick up the slack. Cove has three monitoring centers, which is still solid redundancy, but six is better when we're talking about the system responsible for calling 911 on your behalf.

One thing worth mentioning about ADT: they're the largest and oldest home security provider in the country. They've been around for over 150 years. Some people get real peace of mind just from that track record. It costs more than Cove, but a lot of buyers feel better knowing they're with a company that's been doing this longer than anyone else.

During testing, both systems sent alerts quickly after an alarm was triggered. A monitoring agent called within about 30 seconds if I didn't respond to the initial notification. Both companies were on par with the industry average for response times.

### Cove Monitoring Plans

Cove keeps pricing simple. The Basic plan is $23 per month and covers 24/7 professional monitoring for police, fire, and medical, plus the app on your phone. If you want camera access, voice assistant integration with Alexa and Google, a lifetime equipment warranty, and a $5 per month equipment credit, the Plus plan is $33 per month. That $5 credit accumulates on your account and can be used toward buying more Cove equipment, so your real out-of-pocket cost on the Plus plan is closer to $28 per month.

### ADT Monitoring Plans

ADT's monitoring starts at $42.99 per month for core security and smart home access. If your system includes cameras, you're looking at $48.99 per month, which bundles in a Google Home Premium subscription for cloud storage and video features. ADT's plans are more expensive than Cove's, but you're also getting smart home automation, straight cellular monitoring, and access to six monitoring centers.

### Contracts

Cove is month-to-month. No contract, period. You can cancel whenever you want with no penalty. That's a big deal if you're not sure about committing long-term or if you're a renter who might move in a year.

ADT requires a minimum 12-month agreement if you purchase equipment upfront. If you finance the equipment instead, you're looking at a 36-month commitment. Canceling early means paying 75% of your remaining balance. That's a real consideration, especially if you think there's any chance you'll move or switch systems within the next few years.

### Monitoring Verdict

ADT has the stronger monitoring infrastructure with cellular-first connections and six monitoring centers. Cove has the better pricing and flexibility with no contract. If monitoring reliability is your top priority, ADT wins. If you want to save money and avoid commitments, Cove is the better deal.

## Cove and ADT Camera Comparison

Here's something both systems have going for them: neither uses proprietary cameras. ADT partners with Google Nest. Cove partners with Eufy. That means if you ever cancel either service, you can still use your cameras through the Google Home app (ADT) or the Eufy app (Cove). You don't lose your investment. That's a genuine advantage over companies like Vivint, where canceling means your cameras are basically decorative.

Both camera systems record in HD, have night vision, two-way talk, and motion-triggered recording. You can pull up live feeds from either app and get push notifications when something triggers.

### Cove Cameras

Cove's cameras are made by Eufy. You get a doorbell camera, an indoor camera, and an outdoor camera with a built-in solar panel. The outdoor camera is completely wireless and charges itself via solar. I live in Central Florida, so we get plenty of sun and I had no issues keeping it charged. If you're in a climate with less sun, that's something to consider.

<figure>
  <Image src={coveSoloCam} alt="Eufy SoloCam held in hand showing solar panel" />
  <figcaption>Cove's outdoor camera. Charges via the solar panel on top.</figcaption>
</figure>

One pro I don't see a lot of people talking about: each Cove camera has an SD card slot, so even if your Wi-Fi goes out, the cameras keep recording locally. You won't have remote access through the app until Wi-Fi comes back, but the footage is still being captured and stored on the card. That's a real safety net during a power outage or if someone cuts your internet.

<figure>
  <Image src={coveOutdoorNight} alt="Cove outdoor camera night vision view of pool area" />
  <figcaption>Night vision on the Cove outdoor camera. Solid quality for the price.</figcaption>
</figure>

The downside: Cove cameras only record motion-triggered video clips. There's no option for 24/7 continuous recording. And the indoor camera doesn't distinguish between people and objects, so if your cat walks by or a curtain blows, it triggers a recording. That gets annoying when you're scrolling through clips looking for something specific.

I also had some frustration getting the outdoor camera connected during initial setup. It took longer than expected. But once it was connected, everything ran smoothly from that point on.

### ADT Cameras

ADT uses Google Nest cameras, and the video quality is a step up from Cove. The recordings are clearer, and the Nest cameras use AI to distinguish between people, animals, packages, and vehicles. That means fewer false alerts and more relevant notifications. When someone walks up to your front door, it tells you it's a person. When a squirrel runs across the porch, it doesn't light up your phone.

<figure>
  <Image src={adtNestDoorbell} alt="Google Nest doorbell camera installed at front door" />
  <figcaption>The Nest doorbell camera at my front door. Clean footage and smart alerts.</figcaption>
</figure>

The Nest cameras can run on battery or be plugged in. That gives you flexibility for spots without a nearby outlet, like a shed out back or a side gate. Cove's outdoor camera also handles this well with the solar charging, but the Nest cameras give you the option to just plug in and forget about it.

<figure>
  <Image src={adtNestCamOutdoor} alt="ADT Nest outdoor camera daytime footage" />
  <figcaption>Daytime footage from the Nest outdoor camera. Clear and detailed.</figcaption>
</figure>

ADT also offers 24/7 continuous recording as an add-on for $10 per month through the Google Home subscription. If you want every minute recorded, not just motion clips, that option exists with ADT. Cove doesn't offer it at all.

The one downside: the Nest cameras only record up to one hour without Wi-Fi. After that, they stop. Compare that to Cove's cameras, which keep recording to the SD card indefinitely without Wi-Fi. If you live in an area with unreliable internet, that's a point in Cove's favor.

My other gripe with ADT is the two-app situation. The ADT+ app handles arming, disarming, and smart home controls. But if you want to download recorded video, you have to jump over to the Google Home app. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a little annoying. Cove keeps everything under one app.

### Camera Verdict

ADT's Nest cameras have better video quality, AI detection, and the option for continuous recording. Cove's cameras are significantly cheaper, record locally without Wi-Fi (no time limit), and you can add unlimited cameras without increasing your monthly bill. If camera quality is your priority, go with ADT. If you want affordable cameras that keep recording no matter what, Cove is the better pick.

## DIY vs Professional Installation

This is one of the biggest differences between these two systems.

### Setup Cove

Cove is completely DIY. You plug in the base station and panel, connect them, and the panel walks you through a series of prompts to pair your sensors and cameras. The sensors use 3M adhesive strips. Peel off the backing, stick them on your doors and windows, done. No drilling, no tools, nothing complicated.

<figure>
  <Image src={coveDoorSensor} alt="Installing a Cove door sensor above a door frame" />
  <figcaption>Installing a Cove door sensor. Peel the adhesive, stick it on, done.</figcaption>
</figure>

The sensors and panel took me about 30 minutes. The cameras added more time because I had trouble getting the outdoor camera to connect. All in, the setup took me about three hours. Once everything was connected, I didn't have any issues with things dropping off.

<figure>
  <Image src={coveAdhesive} alt="Peeling 3M adhesive backing off a Cove sensor" />
  <figcaption>The 3M adhesive strips peel off cleanly when you move. Renter-friendly.</figcaption>
</figure>

If installing a security system sounds intimidating, don't overthink it with Cove. The process is approachable even if you've never done it before. And since everything is wireless and adhesive-mounted, the whole system is renter-friendly. When you move out, peel the sensors off and take them with you. No holes, no damage.

### Setup ADT

ADT sends a technician to your house who handles everything. They connected all the sensors, set up the panel, tested the system, and installed the outdoor camera. The camera installation alone was worth it. Running wires from the outside of the house to the inside, drilling through walls, making sure everything is sealed and weatherproofed. I wouldn't want to do that myself.

<figure>
  <Image src={adtDoorSensor} alt="ADT door sensor installed on door frame" />
  <figcaption>ADT door sensor installed by the technician. Clean and barely noticeable.</figcaption>
</figure>

My ADT installation took about two to three hours. The technician was thorough and walked me through how to use the app, arm and disarm the system, and set up automations. Having someone do it in person gives you a level of confidence that it's all set up right the first time.

There's also a real advantage if you're moving into a house that already has an existing hardwired security system. ADT can often take over those existing sensors and integrate them into your new setup. They can even use some wireless sensors from other companies like Honeywell. That can save you a lot on equipment since you're not buying everything from scratch. Cove only works with Cove sensors, so you're starting fresh regardless.

ADT's professional installation starts at $99, though they frequently waive the fee through promotions. <a href={adtCompanyData.website}>Check ADT's current installation deals here</a>.

### Installation Verdict

If you want hands-off setup done by a pro, ADT is the clear choice. If you're comfortable with DIY and want to save money, Cove's installation is straightforward and renter-friendly.

## ADT Smart Home Automation

This is where ADT pulls way ahead. Cove doesn't do home automation at all. It handles security, cameras, and that's it. No smart locks, no thermostat control, no light switches. It's a traditional alarm system.

ADT connects to anything Z-Wave compatible. Door locks, thermostats, garage door openers, light switches. Everything shows up in the ADT+ app alongside your security system and cameras. One app, one hub, everything under one roof.

<figure>
  <Image src={adtSmartLock} alt="ADT smart lock controlled from app" />
  <figcaption>The ADT smart lock. Lock and unlock from the app or the keypad.</figcaption>
</figure>

You can also set up automations. Monday through Friday at 9 PM, automatically arm the alarm, lock the front door, and turn off the lights. When you set the alarm to Away mode, lock all doors and lower the thermostat. I set these up once and haven't thought about them since.

If smart home features matter to you at all, Cove can't compete in this category. ADT is the only option here.

Both apps work remotely from anywhere with cell service. You don't need to be on your home Wi-Fi.

## Cove vs ADT Pricing And Costs

### Upfront ADT Equipment Costs

My ADT system cost me a little over $400 for all the equipment, buying everything upfront. ADT equipment packages generally range from $250 to $800 depending on how many cameras and devices you add. Financing is available, and you can roll the equipment cost into your monthly bill over 36 months. Just know that financing means you're locked into the monitoring service until the balance is paid off.

### Upfront Cove Equipment Costs

I got Cove's 70% off deal with a free indoor camera and paid $250.48 for everything. That included three cameras, the panel, door and window sensors, a motion sensor, and a glass break sensor. Without the discount, Cove's starter kits typically range from $120 to $300 depending on the configuration. Either way, Cove is significantly cheaper upfront.

### Monthly Monitoring Costs

| Plan | Cove | ADT |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core security monitoring | $23/mo | $42.99/mo |
| Security + cameras | $33/mo ($28 effective) | $48.99/mo |
| Smart home + cameras | N/A | $48.99/mo |

Cove's Plus plan ($33/mo) includes a $5 monthly equipment credit that accumulates on your account, bringing your effective cost down to about $28 per month. That credit can be used to buy additional Cove equipment down the line.

ADT starts at $42.99 for monitoring and smart home access. Adding cameras bumps it to $48.99, which includes a Google Home Premium subscription. ADT is roughly $20 more per month than Cove, which adds up to $240 per year.

### Pricing Verdict

Cove wins on price by a wide margin. Lower upfront equipment costs, lower monthly monitoring, and no contract. If you're price-conscious and want reliable home security without paying a premium, Cove is the better deal.

## Which Home Security System Should You Get?

After testing both, here's where I land.

### Get Cove If You Want Affordable Home Security

Cove is the right pick if you want solid security without spending a fortune. The upfront cost is dramatically lower, the monthly cost is about half of what ADT charges, there's no contract, and the cameras aren't proprietary. If you ever cancel, you keep your Eufy cameras and just download the Eufy app.

Cove doesn't have smart home features or the camera quality that ADT offers. But for the price, it's hard to beat. The professional monitoring is reliable, the touchscreen panel has two-way voice, the cameras record locally even without Wi-Fi, and you get everything you need to protect your home.

If you're a renter, Cove is especially appealing. Everything is wireless, nothing requires drilling, and you can take the entire system with you when you move. The month-to-month plan means you're never locked into anything.

### The Bottom Line

For budget-conscious buyers who want reliable home security with no strings attached, Cove is the move. Low upfront cost, low monthly cost, no contract, and your cameras keep working even if you cancel.

For homeowners who want the full smart home security experience with professional installation and monitoring, ADT is worth the premium. You're paying more, but you're getting more in return: better cameras, smart home automation, cellular-first monitoring, and the peace of mind that comes with having the largest security company in the country backing your system.

Either way, both companies offer 24/7 professional monitoring to police, fire, and medical. You're making a solid choice with either one. It really comes down to how much you want to spend and whether smart home features matter to you.
