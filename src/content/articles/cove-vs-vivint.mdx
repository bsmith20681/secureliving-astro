## How Cove and Vivint Handle Monitoring

Both systems connect to professional monitoring centers that dispatch police, fire, and medical 24/7. But there's a difference in how each system communicates with the monitoring center.

Cove connects through your home Wi-Fi and uses cellular as a backup. So if your internet goes down, it'll fall back to cellular and you'll still have service. Vivint, on the other hand, goes straight to cellular as the primary connection. That means if your power goes out and your internet drops, Vivint is already on cellular without needing to switch over.

I didn't have any issues with either system during my testing, but the Vivint approach gives me a little more confidence. If there's ever a break-in and your internet connection is spotty, Cove has to try Wi-Fi first, fail, then switch to cellular. Vivint just calls out immediately. That could matter in a real emergency.

Both systems also have backup batteries in the base station, so if the power goes out, your system stays active for up to 24 hours.

One thing worth mentioning: Vivint can actually take over existing hardwired sensors from other security companies. If you moved into a house that already has sensors installed from a previous system, Vivint's technician can often integrate those into your new system. They can also use some wireless sensors from other brands in certain situations. That can save you a decent amount of money on equipment since you're not buying a whole new set of sensors from scratch. Cove only works with Cove sensors, so you'd be starting fresh regardless.

Overall, both of these security systems have solid monitoring. I lean slightly toward Vivint here because the cellular-first approach feels more reliable, and the ability to use existing sensors is a nice bonus. But compared to other DIY systems like SimpliSafe or Ring, Cove's monitoring features are more robust. The two-way voice through the panel is a genuine safety feature that most DIY companies don't include.

## Cove and Vivint Camera Comparison

Both camera systems come with HD video, night vision, two-way talk, motion alerts, and video clip recording. You can pull up live feeds from either app and get push notifications when activity is detected. The basics are covered on both sides.

### Cove Cameras

Cove partners with Eufy for their cameras. You get a doorbell camera, indoor camera, and an outdoor camera with a solar panel built in. The outdoor camera is completely wireless, which is great if you don't have an outlet nearby. I live in central Florida, so we get plenty of sun, and I had no issues keeping the solar panel charged.

The biggest advantage with Cove's cameras is that they're non-proprietary. Since they're Eufy cameras, if you ever cancel your Cove monitoring service, you can download the Eufy app and keep using your cameras for free. That's a big deal if you're not sure about long-term commitment.

<figure>
  <Image src={coveOutdoorNight} alt="Cove outdoor camera night vision view" />
  <figcaption>Night vision on the Cove outdoor camera looking at my pool area.</figcaption>

The downside? Cove only records video clips triggered by motion. There's no 24/7 continuous recording option. And the video quality, while solid for the price, isn't as sharp as what you get with Vivint's cameras. The indoor camera in particular can't distinguish between people and objects, so if a curtain blows in the breeze or your cat walks by, it triggers a recording. That can make it tedious to sort through clips when you're looking for something specific.

I did have some trouble getting the outdoor camera to connect during initial setup. It was frustrating, but once I got it working, everything stayed connected and ran smoothly from that point on.

### Vivint Cameras

Vivint's cameras are on a different level. The video quality is noticeably better. Recordings are clearer and higher resolution, and you can tell the difference immediately when comparing footage side by side. Vivint's cameras also use AI to distinguish between people, animals, packages, and vehicles. That means you get fewer false alerts and more relevant notifications. When someone walks up to your front door, the camera knows it's a person and tells you. When your neighbor's cat wanders through your yard, it doesn't blow up your phone.

<figure>
  <Image src={vivintOutdoorCam} alt="Vivint outdoor camera mounted on house" />

Vivint cameras are always plugged in, and the technician handles all the wiring during installation. You don't have to worry about climbing a ladder or drilling holes through your exterior wall. The trade-off is that if you want a camera somewhere without an outlet (like a shed in the backyard), Vivint's cameras aren't going to work for that.

The cameras also support Power over Ethernet, which means you can plug them directly into your router for a more stable connection and even better video quality. If you've ever dealt with wireless security cameras that keep disconnecting, that feature alone might be worth the upgrade.

And then there's Vivint's deter mode, which is probably my favorite camera feature of any system I've tested. When the cameras detect motion in deter mode, they play a whistling sound to grab the person's attention. The LED ring around the camera lens lights up. And if you have the outdoor camera with the floodlight attachment, the floodlight blasts on and illuminates your entire yard. It's enough to make anyone think twice about sticking around. Most cameras just record and watch. Vivint's cameras actually try to prevent the crime from happening, which is something I haven't seen any other company do this well.

If you want 24/7 continuous recording (not just clips), Vivint offers that as an add-on for $7 per month. Cove doesn't have that option at all.

The downside with Vivint's cameras: they're proprietary. If you cancel Vivint monitoring, you lose access to the Vivint app and can't view your cameras remotely anymore. You can still view them from the keypad in your home, but that's it. That's a real consideration if you think there's any chance you might switch systems down the road. With Cove, your cameras are still useful after canceling. With Vivint, they're basically decorative.

Vivint also offers local storage options. They sell a DVR that can store up to 10 days of 24/7 video recording, and most cameras have SD card slots as well. Cloud storage keeps video clips and photos for up to 30 days. Cove's cameras also support local storage via microSD cards (up to 128GB, which gets you roughly 120 hours of footage), and cloud storage is available through the Eufy ecosystem.

## DIY vs Professional Installation

This is one of the biggest differences between these two systems. Cove is completely DIY. Vivint is professionally installed.

### Installing Cove

Cove's installation is about as easy as it gets. You plug in the base station and the keypad, connect them, and then the panel walks you through a series of prompts to pair up your sensors and cameras. The sensors use 3M adhesive strips, so you're just peeling off the backing and sticking them on your doors and windows. No drilling, no tools, nothing complicated.

<figure>
  <Image src={coveDoorSensor} alt="Installing a Cove door sensor above a door frame" />
  <figcaption>Installing a Cove door sensor. Peel off the adhesive, stick it on, done.</figcaption>
</figure>

I did have some frustration getting the outdoor camera connected. It took me longer than expected, and I go into more detail about that in my <a href="/articles/cove-review">full Cove review</a>. But once everything was connected, I had no issues with things disconnecting.

If installing a security system on your own sounds intimidating, don't overthink it. Cove makes the process pretty hard to mess up. And since everything is wireless and uses adhesive, the whole system is renter-friendly. When you move out, you peel the sensors off and take them with you. No holes in the walls, no damage to the door frames.

Cove also offers professional installation starting at $129 if you really don't want to do it yourself. But honestly, save the money. If you can use a smartphone, you can install Cove.

### Installing Vivint

Vivint sends a technician to your house, and they handle everything. They install the panel, mount the cameras, run the wiring, set up the door lock and thermostat, and integrate any existing sensors you might already have in your home. Once they're done, they walk you through how to use everything.

My installation took about three to four hours. The technician was thorough, and I got a lot of peace of mind knowing the cameras were installed correctly. These cameras need to be plugged in, so there's wiring involved. Drilling through exterior walls, routing cables, making sure everything is sealed up and weatherproofed. That's not a DIY-friendly project for most people, and I wouldn't want to do it myself.

The technician also set up my Nest thermostat, programmed the smart lock, connected everything to the Vivint app, and showed me how to use it all before leaving. Having someone walk you through the system in person is genuinely helpful, especially if you've never owned a home security system before. They answer questions, make sure your sensors are placed in the right spots, and test everything before they leave.

If you plan on using existing hardwired sensors from a previous security system, I'd definitely recommend having a professional do it. Taking over a hardwired system can get technical, and Vivint's technicians know what they're doing.

## Vivint Smart Home Automation

This is where Vivint pulls way ahead. Cove doesn't do home automation at all. It's core security only: monitoring, sensors, cameras, and that's it.

Vivint, on the other hand, is a full smart home system. Anything that's Z-Wave compatible can connect to Vivint. That means door locks, light switches, thermostats, garage door openers, and more. You can control all of them from the Vivint app alongside your security system and cameras. Everything under one roof, one app.

Or when someone unlocks the front door, disarm the alarm and turn on the hallway lights. There's a lot of flexibility, and it turns your house into a real smart home without having to Frankenstein together a bunch of different apps and devices.

## How They Handle Emergencies

Both Cove and Vivint use professional monitoring centers that respond to security, fire, and medical alerts around the clock. When an alarm triggers, the monitoring center gets notified and attempts to contact you through the panel's two-way voice and by calling your phone. If you don't respond or confirm it's a real emergency, they dispatch the appropriate first responders.

Cove uses RapidSOS technology, which sends precise location data and alarm details directly to 911 dispatchers. That cuts response time because the monitoring agents don't have to verbally relay your address and situation over the phone. Cove also has three monitoring centers across the U.S. for redundancy, so even if one center goes down, another picks up the slack.

Vivint's monitoring center sends text alerts to you and your listed contacts. That's useful if you're on vacation and have someone housesitting. They'll get the alerts too and can check on things. During my testing, Vivint's team responded quickly when an alarm was triggered, calling to confirm the situation before taking action.

Both companies have solid customer service as well. Phone support is responsive (I got through to someone within a few minutes with both companies), and both offer web chat that connects you to a real person, not a bot. I didn't have any complaints with either company's support team.

## Cove vs Vivint Pricing And Contracts

### Upfront Equipment Costs

Cove is significantly cheaper upfront. I got the 70% off deal with a free indoor camera and paid $250.48 for my entire system, which included three cameras, the panel, sensors, motion sensor, and glass break sensor.

Vivint starts at around $200 if you just want core security. But I went all out and bought everything: cameras, panel, door lock, thermostat, floodlight. My total was a little over $1,000. You definitely don't need to spend that much, but if you want the full smart home experience, expect to pay more than you would with Cove.

Vivint does offer 0% APR financing for 42 or 60 months, which spreads out that upfront cost into your monthly bill. That makes it a lot more manageable. Just know that if you finance the equipment, you're obligated to keep the monitoring service until the equipment balance is paid off. If you want to cancel early, you just pay the remaining balance on the equipment and you're free to go.

Something worth noting: Vivint's equipment costs start much lower if you don't need all the extras. A basic security setup with the panel, sensors, and a camera can start around $200. I spent over $1,000 because I wanted every device they offer, but that's definitely not the norm. Most people will land somewhere in the $400-$600 range depending on how many cameras and smart home devices they add.

Cove keeps it simple. $23 per month covers core security monitoring. $33 per month adds camera access, voice control, a lifetime warranty, and a $5 per month equipment credit that accumulates on your account. So your real out-of-pocket cost is about $28 per month for the full plan.

Vivint starts at $24.99 for core security. If you want smart home features, that goes up to $39 per month. Each camera is an additional $5 per month. I have the security system with smart home and two cameras, so I'm paying $49 per month.

### Contracts

Both companies technically offer month-to-month service. With Cove, there's no contract, period. You can cancel whenever you want with no penalty.

With Vivint, it's month-to-month if you pay for the equipment upfront. But if you choose to finance the equipment (which most people do), you're committed to paying for monitoring as long as you have a balance. The financing can run up to 60 months, so that's potentially a five-year commitment. The silver lining is that Vivint locks in your monthly price for the life of the agreement. So even if they raise prices for new customers, your rate stays the same. That's a nice buffer if you're planning to keep the system long-term.

If you ever want to cancel entirely, just pay off the remaining equipment balance and you're done. No separate cancellation fee on top of it.

### Get Cove If You Want Affordable Core Security

Is Cove a good security system? Absolutely. The professional monitoring is reliable, the touchscreen keypad has two-way voice, the cameras record locally even without Wi-Fi, and you get everything you need to protect your home. It doesn't have smart home features or the premium camera quality that Vivint offers, but for the price, it's hard to beat.

If you're a renter, Cove is especially appealing. Everything is wireless, nothing requires drilling, and you can take the entire system with you when you move. The month-to-month plan with no contract means you're not locked into anything, and if you ever cancel, your Eufy cameras still work through the free Eufy app.
