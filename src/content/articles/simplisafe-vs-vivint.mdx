I've reviewed both SimpliSafe and Vivint individually, so I wanted to put them head to head and compare everything side by side. These two security systems take very different approaches to home security, and the right pick depends on what you care about most.

The quick take: Vivint is the better system. Hands down. It has a touchscreen panel, professional installation, straight cellular monitoring, smart home automation, and cameras that record without Wi-Fi. But it costs more and financing locks you into a service agreement. SimpliSafe is the more affordable DIY option that still covers the security basics, and its Active Guard feature (where a live person monitors your cameras) is something you won't find at this price point anywhere else.

Here's the full comparison.

## SimpliSafe vs Vivint Monitoring And Connection

Both systems offer professional monitoring that dispatches police, fire, and medical. But the way each system communicates with the monitoring center is different, and it matters.

SimpliSafe connects through your home Wi-Fi first and uses cellular as a backup. If your internet goes down, it falls back to cellular and you still have service. Vivint goes straight to cellular. It doesn't rely on your Wi-Fi at all to contact the monitoring station. That means if your power goes out and your internet drops, Vivint is already on cellular without needing to switch over. That can result in a slightly quicker response time because there's no handoff between connections.

The other difference is two-way voice on the panel. Vivint's touchscreen panel has a built-in speaker and microphone, so if the alarm goes off, a monitoring agent can communicate with you through the panel itself. If a break-in is happening and you're inside the house, you're probably not in a position to answer your phone. The agent can hear what's going on through the panel and relay that to the police. SimpliSafe doesn't have this. Their monitoring agents can only reach you by calling your phone.

One more thing worth mentioning: Vivint can often use existing equipment from a previous home security system. If you moved into a house that already has hardwired sensors or even some wireless sensors from other companies like Honeywell, Vivint's technicians can take over and integrate those into your new system. That saves you real money on equipment since you're not starting from scratch. SimpliSafe only works with SimpliSafe sensors. No exceptions.

### SimpliSafe Monitoring Plans

SimpliSafe's monitoring starts at $32.99 per month, which covers professional monitoring to police, fire, and medical, plus the app and camera access with cloud storage. If you want their Active Guard feature (a live agent watching your outdoor cameras between 8 PM and 6 AM), that bumps up to $49.99 per month. And if you want Active Guard running 24/7, it's $79.99 per month. That's expensive, but it's a feature most security systems don't offer at all, so there's not much to compare it against.

SimpliSafe also has a free self-monitoring tier where you get the app and can arm/disarm remotely, but you don't get professional monitoring or cellular backup. I wouldn't recommend it. Without professional monitoring, you're relying on yourself to call 911, and without cellular backup, a power outage takes out your whole system.

### Vivint Monitoring Plans

Vivint starts at $29.99 per month for core security monitoring plus the app. If you want smart home features (automations, thermostat control, etc.), it goes up to $39.99. Add cameras on top of that and you're looking at $49.99 per month, plus $5 for each additional camera beyond the first. My Vivint bill comes to $49 per month because I have two cameras plus smart home plus security monitoring.

### SimpliSafe vs Vivint Contracts

Both companies technically offer month-to-month service. With SimpliSafe, it's straightforward. No contract, period. Cancel whenever you want, no penalty.

Vivint is a little more complicated. If you pay for all your equipment upfront, it is month-to-month. But most people finance because Vivint equipment is expensive. If you finance (0% APR for 42 or 60 months), you're obligated to keep the monitoring service active as long as you have a balance on the equipment. So while Vivint calls it "no contract," financing effectively creates one. If you want to cancel early, you need to pay off whatever you owe on the equipment first.

I paid for my SimpliSafe equipment upfront and my Vivint equipment upfront because I'm not a fan of being locked in. But I get why people finance with Vivint. Dropping $1,000+ on equipment all at once isn't realistic for everyone.

### Monitoring Verdict

Vivint has the stronger monitoring infrastructure with straight cellular and two-way voice on the panel. SimpliSafe has more flexible plans and the unique Active Guard feature. If monitoring reliability is your top priority, Vivint wins. If you want the option of live human camera monitoring, SimpliSafe has something no one else offers at this price.

## SimpliSafe And Vivint Camera Comparison

Both systems offer high-definition cameras with night vision, motion detection, two-way talk, and live viewing from the app. The basics are covered on both sides. But there are some real differences once you look closer.

One thing both systems have in common, and I don't love it: both use proprietary cameras. If you cancel SimpliSafe, your cameras stop working. Same with Vivint. That's a real downside for both companies. Compare that to ADT (which uses Google Nest cameras) or Cove (which uses Eufy cameras), where you keep your cameras working even after canceling. It's something to consider if you think there's any chance you'll switch systems down the road.

### SimpliSafe Cameras

SimpliSafe offers an indoor camera, outdoor camera, and doorbell camera. The outdoor camera can run on battery or be plugged in, which is nice if you want to stick a camera on a shed out back where there's no outlet nearby. The battery flexibility gives you options that Vivint can't match in certain spots around the house.

  <Image src={simplisafeOutdoorDay} alt="SimpliSafe outdoor camera daytime pool view" />

SimpliSafe cameras record motion-triggered video clips. There's no option for 24/7 continuous recording. When something triggers the camera, it records a clip anywhere from 10 to 90 seconds long, and that clip gets stored in the cloud for 30 days.

<figure>
  <Image src={simplisafeOutdoorNight} alt="SimpliSafe outdoor camera night vision driveway view" />
  <figcaption>Night vision on the SimpliSafe outdoor camera.</figcaption>
</figure>

The biggest downside to SimpliSafe cameras: they stop recording entirely if your Wi-Fi goes down. No Wi-Fi, no video, period. That's a problem if someone cuts your internet or your power goes out during a storm. You lose camera coverage right when you need it most.

I also noticed some stuttering when live streaming the outdoor camera feed. When I walked in front of it and watched the live view, the video was jittery and jumping around. The downloaded clips looked fine, but the live stream wasn't great. That's likely a Wi-Fi bandwidth issue, but it's worth calling out.

Where SimpliSafe really stands out is Active Guard. This is their live video monitoring feature where an actual person at the monitoring station watches your camera feeds. If the outdoor camera picks up motion, the agent can see what's happening and talk to the person through the camera's speaker. If someone's peeking through your windows at 2 AM, that agent can say something and even dispatch police based on what they're seeing. Traditional security systems wait until someone kicks down the door to trip the alarm. Active Guard can potentially stop a crime before it happens.

That service is $49 to $79 per month depending on whether you want overnight or 24/7 coverage. It's pricey, but it's a genuinely unique feature.

### Vivint Cameras

Vivint offers an indoor camera, outdoor camera (with an optional floodlight attachment), and a doorbell camera. The cameras need to be plugged in. No battery option. That means you're drilling holes and running wires for outdoor cameras, which is why professional installation matters here.

The video quality is a step up from SimpliSafe. The connection is more stable, the live stream is smoother, and the recordings are clearer. Vivint also offers a power-over-ethernet option, which means you can plug the camera directly into your modem for an even more reliable connection. When I compared the live feeds side by side, Vivint's cameras were noticeably smoother than SimpliSafe's.

<figure>

Vivint cameras keep recording even without Wi-Fi. If your internet goes down, the cameras store footage locally and you can view recordings directly from the Vivint panel. SimpliSafe cameras stop recording entirely without Wi-Fi. That's a big difference.

Vivint also offers 24/7 continuous recording as an add-on for $7 per month. If you want every minute recorded, not just motion clips, that option exists. SimpliSafe doesn't offer it at all.

Vivint's version of deterrence is automated rather than human-monitored. When the cameras detect motion in deter mode, they play a whistle sound, flash an LED ring around the camera lens, and (with the floodlight attachment) light up your entire yard. It's designed to grab the intruder's attention and make them look directly at the camera. I know if I were in their shoes, I wouldn't be sticking around.

### Camera Verdict

Vivint has better video quality, a more stable connection, records without Wi-Fi, and offers continuous recording. SimpliSafe cameras have battery flexibility and the unique Active Guard live monitoring feature. If camera quality and reliability matter most, go with Vivint. If you want a live person watching your cameras, SimpliSafe is the only game in town at this price.

## DIY vs Professional Installation

This is one of the biggest differences between these two home security systems.

### Installing SimpliSafe

SimpliSafe is completely DIY. You unbox everything, download the app, plug in the base station, and follow the on-screen prompts to pair your sensors and cameras. The sensors use peel-and-stick adhesive. Pull off the backing, stick it on the door frame, done. No drilling, no tools.

<figure>
  <Image src={simplisafeEntrySensor} alt="Installing a SimpliSafe entry sensor on a door" />
  <figcaption>Installing a SimpliSafe entry sensor. Peel the backing, stick it on.</figcaption>
</figure>

The whole process took me about two to three hours. The sensors and base station were simple. The cameras added some time but nothing too bad. Out of all the DIY security systems I've reviewed, SimpliSafe did the best job making the process approachable. If you've never installed a security system before, you'll be fine here.

Since everything is wireless and adhesive-mounted, SimpliSafe is renter-friendly. When you move out, peel the sensors off and take everything with you. No holes, no damage, no reinstallation fee.

### Installing Vivint

Vivint sends a technician to your house who handles everything. They connected all my sensors, set up the panel, tested the system, and installed the outdoor camera with the floodlight. The outdoor camera installation alone was worth it. Running wires from outside to inside, drilling through the wall, sealing everything up. I wouldn't want to do that myself.

<figure>
  <Image src={vivintPanelArmed} alt="Vivint panel armed and ready" />

My Vivint installation took about two to three hours as well. The technician walked me through the app, showed me how to arm and disarm, and explained how to set up automations. There's a level of confidence that comes with knowing a professional who installs these systems all day long set everything up correctly the first time.

And like I mentioned earlier, if your house already has an existing hardwired security system from a previous owner, Vivint's technician can integrate those sensors into your new setup. That's a real advantage if you're moving into a house that already has wiring in place. SimpliSafe can't do that.

If you want hands-off setup done by a pro (especially for outdoor cameras), Vivint is the way to go. If you're comfortable with DIY and want to save money, SimpliSafe's installation is straightforward and renter-friendly.

## Vivint Smart Home Automation

This is where Vivint pulls way ahead. SimpliSafe barely does home automation at all. It works with an automatic deadbolt attachment that lets you lock and unlock your door from the app and use a PIN code. That's it. No thermostat control, no light switches, no Z-Wave support, no automations beyond the door lock.

Vivint connects to anything Z-Wave compatible. Door locks, thermostats, garage door openers, light switches. Everything shows up in the Vivint app alongside your security system and cameras. One app, one hub, everything in one place.

You can also set up automations. Monday through Friday at 9 PM, automatically arm the alarm, lock the front door, and turn off the lights. Or when you set the system to Away mode, have it lock all doors and lower the thermostat. Each device can act as a trigger that fires off other actions. You can get creative with it and turn your house into a real smart home.

If smart home features matter to you at all, SimpliSafe can't compete in this category. Vivint is the only option here.

My SimpliSafe system had a retail price of about $965. I was able to get 50% off through a promotion and paid $530 for all the equipment. Just remember that financing means you're committed to the monitoring service for the length of the loan.

### The Bottom Line

For budget-conscious buyers who want solid home security with no strings attached (and maybe live camera monitoring), SimpliSafe is the move. Lower monthly cost, no contract, easy DIY setup.

For homeowners who want the full smart home security package with professional installation, a touchscreen panel, straight cellular monitoring, and home automation, Vivint is worth the premium. You're paying more, but you're getting a lot more in return.
